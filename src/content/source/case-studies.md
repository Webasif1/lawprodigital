# Law Pro Digital — Case Study Library (Long-form)

Seventeen long-form, story-driven case studies: sixteen from one anonymized multi-office US immigration law firm and one from a named Maryland bankruptcy firm (Story 17), written for the people who decide: managing partners and firm owners. Each page tells the story, explains the strategy and why it works for law firms, and follows the numbers all the way to signed clients.

## How to build each page

A managing partner reading a case study asks four questions, in this order: *Is this firm like mine? Did it produce signed clients, not just clicks? How exactly did they do it? Can I trust these numbers?* Every page below answers them in that order.

  

**Page layout (top to bottom)**

  

1.  **Hero:** H1 with the result, one-line subhead, hero image.
2.  **The short answer (AEO block):** 2–3 sentences that state the result and the method. Put it in a highlighted box. Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT and Perplexity lift this paragraph when someone asks a related question.
3.  **At a glance:** 4 stat cards.
4.  **The story:** The firm → The problem → The strategy and why it works for law firms → How we did it (step by step) → The results, from lead to signed client → What didn't work → What your firm can take from this.
5.  **FAQ:** 3–4 questions with answer-first replies (FAQPage schema).
6.  **CTA block** and the privacy line.

  

**AEO / GEO checklist for every page**

  

  - H1 and H2s phrased the way a lawyer would search or ask ("How do immigration firms get clients from Facebook?").
  - The first sentence under every H2 answers the heading directly, with a number.
  - One entity sentence per page, identical everywhere: *"Law Pro Digital is a digital growth agency for US immigration law firms, specializing in Meta Ads, AEO/GEO and AI-powered client intake."*
  - Author box: your name, photo, role, LinkedIn link, "Last updated" date.
  - Schema: Article + FAQPage + Organization (+ BreadcrumbList). Use the same Organization name, logo and sameAs links (LinkedIn, Facebook, YouTube) on every page.
  - Data in HTML tables, not only in images, so AI tools can read and cite the numbers.
  - Link each case study to 2–3 related ones and to the matching service page.

  

**Image and design direction**

  

  - **Palette:** deep navy (\#0F1F3D), white, soft grey (\#F4F6F9) and one accent, teal (\#14B8A6) or gold (\#C9A227). Use the accent only on the key number.
  - **Hero images:** real-feeling, warm photography: an immigration attorney with a client across a desk, a family holding documents, a professional on a video consultation. Avoid gavels, scales of justice and handshake stock photos.
  - **Data visuals:** clean bar or line charts in navy and grey with the accent on the result; big-number stat cards; simple funnel graphics (Reach → Conversations → Consultations → Signed clients).
  - **Proof images:** blurred screenshots of Ads Manager, Search Console and Google Business Profile dashboards with the client's name hidden. They signal "these numbers are real" without breaking confidentiality.
  - **Mobile first:** stat cards stack 2×2, charts are swipeable, and the CTA button stays visible.
  - Every image gets descriptive alt text (written under each story below), which helps both accessibility and AI search.

  

**Privacy line for every page:** *Client name withheld for confidentiality. Figures come from the firm's Meta Ads Manager, Google Analytics, Search Console, Google Business Profile, call-tracking and client-intake records. Qualified firms can see the live dashboards on their onboarding call.*

### How a multi-office immigration law firm grew from 79 to 115 signed clients a month

*Subhead:* Not more ads. A connected system: be found, be trusted, be easy to reach, and measure everything against signed retainers.

  

**Image (hero):** An immigration attorney in a bright office reviewing documents with a young family; warm daylight, navy and white tones. *Alt text: Immigration attorney meeting a family to discuss their visa application.*

  

**The short answer:** A multi-office US immigration law firm grew from 79 signed clients in December 2025 to an average of 115 a month in Q3 2026 (+45%), signing 915 new clients in nine months. It worked because we connected four things that usually run separately: Meta ads by office and service, SEO and AI-search content, Google Business Profiles, and call and intake tracking, all measured monthly against signed clients.

#### The firm

The client is a US immigration law firm with four US offices, an office in the Middle East and one in South Asia. It handles more than 20 types of cases, from asylum and family petitions to EB-1, EB-2 NIW and investor visas. Its clients live in US cities and in dozens of countries abroad. At the end of 2025 it was signing **79 new clients a month**.

#### The problem we found

When we opened the firm's data, three things stood out:

  

  - **Nobody knew where calls came from.** In January, 197 of 265 calls to the firm had no recorded source.
  - **Interest wasn't turning into inquiries.** The website had strong traffic (the EB-1 page alone drew 58,667 views in 2025), but only 20 organic contact forms arrived in May 2026.
  - **Every channel ran on its own.** Social media, ads, the website and the Google profiles each had activity, but no one compared them against the only number that matters to a law firm: signed retainers.

#### **The strategy, and why it works for law firms**

People don't hire an immigration lawyer the way they buy shoes. They worry, research, ask questions, and choose someone they trust. So we built the plan around the four steps a client actually takes:

  

1.  **Be found** where they look: Google, Google Maps, ChatGPT and Facebook.
2.  **Be trusted** before the first call: attorney-led videos, reviews, answers to real questions.
3.  **Be easy to reach:** one-tap Messenger and WhatsApp, short forms, calls answered and tracked.
4.  **Be measured:** one monthly scorecard from first click to signed client, by office.

  

This works for law firms because a retainer is high-value and the decision is personal. Small improvements at each step multiply: more people find you, more of them trust you, more of them reach out, and you know which effort to repeat.

#### **How we did it**

**Months 1–2 (Jan–Feb): get the foundations right.** We set up call tracking with categories (existing client, prospective client, source) and launched messaging-first Meta campaigns by office, so each office had its own pipeline. Monthly conversations rose from 408 in January to 712 in February.

  

**Months 3–5 (Mar–May): test services, markets and content.** We ran campaigns per service (RFE help, asylum, K-1, EB-2 Visa Bulletin), tested international markets with small budgets, launched webinars, and rewrote service pages in an answer-first style for Google and AI search. Google clicks rose 81% from December to May.

  

**Months 6–9 (Jun–Sep): scale what converts.** We moved budget to the offices and services that produced signed clients, scaled attorney-led video, tagged every organic inquiry by service, and improved the Google profiles. Organic website inquiries rose from 20 in May to 98 in July.

#### What didn't go to plan

Growth wasn't a straight line. January (68 clients) and April–May (89 and 86) were softer months, and a website migration plus Google updates hurt search traffic in mid-2026. The trend held because no single channel carried the firm on its own.

#### What your firm can take from this

  - Measure marketing against **signed clients**, not likes or clicks.
  - Give every office and every major service its own campaign and its own numbers.
  - Make it easy to start a conversation: messaging and WhatsApp beat long forms for immigration clients.
  - Put your attorneys on camera. Trust is the product.

## **Story 2 · The Meta Ads engine: 15,738 conversations and leads**

### **Do Facebook ads work for immigration lawyers? Here's what 117 campaigns taught us**

*Subhead:* How a messaging-first Meta Ads system turned Facebook and Instagram into a steady source of consultations for a multi-office immigration firm.

  

**Image (hero):** A smartphone showing a WhatsApp-style chat ("Hi, I'd like to ask about my green card") with a blurred law office in the background. *Alt text: Prospective client messaging an immigration law firm from a Facebook ad.*

  

**The short answer:** Yes. For a multi-office US immigration law firm, 117 Meta campaigns produced 15,738 conversations and lead-form leads between February and October 2026 at $4.36 each. In the months with office-level data, the four US offices signed one client for roughly every 21 ad conversations. The key was messaging-first ads, organized by office and by service, judged on signed clients.

  

|  |  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| \*\*15,738\*\* | \*\*$4.36\*\* | \*\*1 in 21\*\* | \*\*117\*\* |
| Conversations + leads (Feb–Oct 2026) | Average cost per contact | Ad conversations that became a signed client (US offices) | Campaigns tested |

#### The situation

The firm already had active Facebook pages and was running some message campaigns. What it lacked was structure: no clear way to tell which campaigns produced consultations, which office benefited, or what a new client really cost.

#### **Why most law firm Facebook ads fail**

Most law firm ads send people to a homepage or a long form. Immigration clients don't behave that way. They have a specific, often stressful question ("Can I extend my visa?", "What does this RFE mean?") and they want to ask a person, often in their own language, before they book anything. A form that asks for everything up front loses them.

#### **The strategy: messaging first, organized by office and service**

1.  **Start a conversation, not a form.** Most campaigns used Messenger, WhatsApp and Instagram DM as the first step. One tap, one question, a human reply.
2.  **One campaign per office per service.** Asylum in Office A, RFE help in Office B, K-1 in Office A, and so on. Each had its own budget, audience and message, so we could see what worked where.
3.  **Lead forms where they fit.** Webinars, attorney visits and international markets used instant forms, because those audiences wanted to register, not chat.
4.  **Video that shows the attorney.** Short explainers and attorney-led clips outperformed static graphics.
5.  **Monthly budget moves.** Every month we cut what didn't produce conversations and clients and funded what did.

  

**Why it works for law firms:** a conversation is a pre-consultation. The intake team can qualify, answer basic questions and book the right attorney before anyone pays for a consultation slot.

#### The results, from conversation to client

  - **15,738 contacts:** 13,195 messaging conversations and 2,543 lead-form leads.
  - **From conversations to clients:** in the six months with office-level client data, the four US offices recorded 7,013 ad conversations and signed 332 clients (all sources), about **one signed client for every 21 conversations**.
  - **Volume grew while costs fell:** the busiest office went from 365 to 949 conversations a month while its cost per conversation dropped from $7.23 to as low as $4.66 (see Story 4).

#### What didn't work

Cheap traffic. One EB-1A campaign optimized for clicks bought 80,682 clicks for $479 and produced zero conversations. We stopped buying clicks and optimized every campaign for conversations or leads (see Story 16).

#### What your firm can take from this

  - Use messaging ads as your first step; forms only where people expect to register.
  - Split campaigns by office and service so the numbers tell you something.
  - Judge Meta on signed clients per month, not reach or likes.

#### FAQ

**How much do Facebook ads cost for an immigration law firm?** In this case, $4.36 per conversation or lead on average, ranging from $2.00 in the best month to $7.77 in the most expensive.

  

**Are Messenger and WhatsApp ads better than lead forms for lawyers?** For US service campaigns, messaging produced more real conversations. Lead forms worked better for webinars, events and international audiences.

## Story 3 · Cost per lead lies: budget by signed client

**URL:** /case-study/law-firm-cost-per-client/ **Primary keyword:** law firm cost per client acquisition **SEO title:** Cost per Lead vs Cost per Client: What 4 Law Firm Offices Taught Us | Law Pro Digital **Meta description:** The office with the most expensive leads had the cheapest clients. Real data from a multi-office immigration firm on why law firms should budget by signed client. **Schema:** Article, FAQPage, Organization

### **The office with the most expensive leads signed the cheapest clients**

*Subhead:* Six months of office-level data from a multi-office immigration firm, and why cost per lead is the wrong number to manage.

  

**Image (hero):** Four office "cards" side by side on a desk map of the US, each with a small stat tag. *Alt text: Comparison of four law firm offices by marketing cost per signed client.*

  

**The short answer:** Cost per lead and cost per client can point in opposite directions. In a multi-office US immigration firm, the office with the most expensive ad conversations ($10.44 each) had the lowest Meta spend per signed client ($56), while an office with cheaper conversations ($7.01) cost $264 per client. Budgeting by signed client, not by lead, changes where the money should go.

  

|  |  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| \*\*$56 vs $264\*\* | \*\*18.6%\*\* | \*\*5x\*\* | \*\*$229 → $46\*\* |
| Meta spend per signed client, best vs weakest office | Best office's conversation-to-client rate | Gap in spend per client between offices | Headquarters' spend per client after rebalancing |

#### The question every multi-office firm gets wrong

Most agencies report cost per lead. It's easy to measure and it looks good on a slide. But a law firm doesn't get paid for leads. It gets paid for signed retainers, and offices convert leads into clients at very different rates: different attorneys, different intake teams, different communities.

#### What we did

1.  Matched every month's Meta spend and ad conversations to **the clients each office actually signed**, using the firm's intake records.
2.  Calculated two numbers per office: *cost per conversation* and *Meta spend per signed client*.
3.  Reviewed them together every month and moved budget accordingly.

#### What the numbers showed (Mar–Sep 2026, six months with office-level client data)

|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| \*\*Office\*\* | \*\*Meta spend\*\* | \*\*Ad conversations\*\* | \*\*Cost per conversation\*\* | \*\*Clients signed\*\* | \*\*Meta spend per client\*\* | \*\*Conversation → client\*\* |
| Office A (Northeast) | $25,805 | 4,315 | $5.98 | 149 | $173 | 3.5% |
| Office B (West Coast) | $9,500 | 1,355 | $7.01 | 36 | $264 | 2.7% |
| Headquarters (Mid-Atlantic) | $9,761 | 1,048 | $9.31 | 92 | $106 | 8.8% |
| Office D (Southeast) | $3,079 | 295 | $10.44 | 55 | $56 | 18.6% |

  

*Clients include all sources (ads, referrals, walk-ins), so "spend per client" is Meta spend divided by every client that office signed. It's a planning number, not a claim that every client came from an ad.*

#### The story behind the table

  - **Office D looked like the worst performer** if you only read cost per conversation. In reality, its team turned almost one in five conversations into a client. It deserved protection, not cuts.
  - **Office B looked fine on leads** but converted few of them. More ad money there would mostly have produced conversations, not clients, so its budget stayed lean.
  - **Headquarters** spent $229 per client from March to May. We reduced its spend from June while its signed clients rose (from 10, 11 and 9 a month to 22, 17 and 23), bringing spend per client down to **$46**.
  - **Office A**, the largest market, got the most budget because it combined good volume with a solid conversion rate (Story 4).

#### Why this works for law firms

Your intake team, your attorneys and your local reputation are part of your marketing. Two offices running identical ads will sign very different numbers of clients. When you budget by signed client, you fund the places where marketing and intake work together, and you spot intake problems early.

  

**Image (insight):** Two bar charts side by side, "Cost per conversation" vs "Spend per signed client", where the ranking flips. Office D highlighted in teal. *Alt text: Chart showing the law firm office with the highest cost per lead had the lowest cost per signed client.*

#### What your firm can take from this

  - Ask your agency for **cost per signed client by office**, every month.
  - Don't cut an office because its leads are expensive. Check how many of those leads become clients.
  - When an office has cheap leads but few clients, fix intake before adding budget.

#### FAQ

**What is a good cost per client for an immigration law firm?** It depends on case value. In this firm, Meta spend per signed client ranged from $46 to $264 by office and month, across all client sources.

  

**Why is cost per lead misleading for law firms?** Because offices and intake teams convert leads at very different rates. Here, conversion from ad conversation to client ranged from 2.7% to 18.6%.

  

**How do I measure cost per client?** Match each month's ad spend to the clients your intake system records for the same office and period, then review the trend monthly.

## **Story 5 · International clients at $0.44 per contact**

#### The insight

Employment and investor immigration is global. A software engineer in Lagos or Karachi researching the EB-2 NIW, or a business owner in Dubai comparing E-2 and EB-5, is a real prospective client. Yet very few US firms advertise to them, so ad costs there are a fraction of US costs.

#### The strategy: small tests, the right offer per market

1.  **Test before you commit.** Each new market started with $40–$300.
